O · Why

A simple question, a difficult answer

  • "How much organic carbon is there in the soils of North Africa?" The answer is spread across dozens of institutions.
  • Each country measures with different methods, units and depths.
  • Many data exist only in reports, spreadsheets and personal disks.
  • Without accessible national data, global maps are made about countries, not with countries.

Soils know no borders.
Soil data,
unfortunately, do.

O · Why

Two ways of bringing data together

Central warehouse

Countries send copies of their data to a single database.

The copy is out of date the next day.
Whoever stores the data decides on access.
The country no longer knows who uses what.

Federation

The data stay in the country; each national system answers standard requests.

Always the current version, straight from the source.
The country decides what it publishes, and can change its mind.
It asks for one thing: speaking the same language.

GloSIS chose federation. The rest of this presentation is about that "same language".

O · Why

Why it matters to Tunisia

  • Visibility: national data are found by those who plan, research and fund.
  • A voice of its own: global products use Tunisia's numbers, validated in Tunisia.
  • Comparability: the same codes as neighbouring countries and partners.
  • Less duplication: knowing what already exists before paying for a new survey.

A · What it is

GloSIS in four sentences

  • The Global Soil Information System of FAO's Global Soil Partnership (GSP).
  • A decentralised system: a federation of national or regional nodes, each one a soil information system.
  • Each node publishes its data following agreed exchange standards.
  • The data still belong to the countries; the GSP helps each country build its system.

A · What it is

The GSP soil data policy

  • Approved by the GSP Plenary Assembly.
  • Respects existing ownership rights over the data that are shared.
  • Accepts the restrictions inherited from national data policies.
  • Metadata open by default, even when the data have access conditions.
  • It also applies to those who use the data and create derived products.

Taking part in GloSIS
is not handing over the data.
It is saying that they exist.

Summary; the text in force is in the GSP documents: fao.org/global-soil-partnership

A · What it is

Global products made with the countries

GSOCmap

Global map of soil organic carbon, compiled from national maps.

GSASmap

Global map of salt-affected soils, with the same approach.

GBSmap

Global map of black soils, the richest in organic matter.

A country-driven approach: each country produces its map; the GSP harmonises and assembles. Quality at national level is what the world ends up seeing.

B · Language

An ontology to say the same thing

  • The GloSIS web ontology: a data model published on the web, reusing existing standards and ontologies.
  • It rests on ISO 28258, the standard for the digital exchange of soil data.
  • The code lists are public and maintained in the open.
  • OpenNSIS uses the same model and the same lists in its database.

A value only makes sense with three labels

# "6.2" says nothing. This does:
property:   pH
procedure:  in water, 1:2.5
unit:       pH
depth:      0–30 cm

Each label is a code from an agreed list, the same in Tunis, Jakarta and Rome.

B · Language

What harmonisation gives in return

  • Compare different projects, laboratories and decades within the country.
  • Combine the data of several providers into one national map.
  • Validate on entry: rules per property, country limits, units converted at ingestion.
  • Born connected: what enters the national SIS harmonised already speaks GloSIS, with no conversions later.

Harmonising is not
changing the data.
It is describing them the same way.

C · Connection

The Discovery Hub in three verbs

Harvest

Periodically, it reads the catalogue and the property summary of each node. No raw data.

Check

It tests whether nodes and map layers are alive. What stops answering leaves the results.

Find

Zoom to an area on the map; then filter by keyword or by soil property.

It also accepts ordinary CSW catalogues (GeoNetwork, pyCSW…): standards only, nothing specific to one provider. Working address: http://5.223.62.255:8083/

R · Tunisia

Connecting: one click in the admin panel

1 · Connect

The node switches the federation on, generates its token and registers with the hub.

2 · Approval

The hub confirms that the address answers; its administrator approves the request.

3 · Disconnect

At any time: requests are then refused. Nothing is deleted.

R · Tunisia

The country decides what is shared

  • The federation receives exactly what a visitor sees in the SIS: the published profiles, with the same limits and the same blurring.
  • Control is per project: publish or not, locations only, a limit on profiles, blurring in metres.
  • Maps travel through the public catalogue and the WMS.
  • The federation API reads the database as a read-only user, limited to three views.

There is no "copy for GloSIS".
There is a window, and the country holds the blind.
