O · Why

Data that do not circulate do no work

  • A soil profile costs fieldwork, transport and laboratory. That cost is paid once.
  • The value lies in being used many times: a map, a recommendation, a thesis, another map.
  • Without clear rules, nobody dares to use the data, or to share them.
  • "Ask by e-mail" depends on one person being in the office. It does not scale and leaves no trace.

Collecting is expensive.
Not using costs more.

O · Why

The fears are legitimate, and they have an answer

FearAnswer
"We lose control of our data."The data stay on the country's server; the licence says what others may do.
"They use the data and do not cite us."Attribution is compulsory in every Creative Commons licence.
"Someone makes money from our work."Commercial use can be excluded, or authorised case by case.
"The data have errors and we will be criticised."The metadata state the method, the date and the quality; whoever uses the data knows what they have.
"The points show someone's farm."Share only the locations, blur the coordinates or limit the number of profiles.
Sharing is not all or nothing: there is a scale, and the step is chosen project by project.

O · Why

Profiles and maps are not the same thing

Soil profiles: the capital

Point data, expensive to obtain and sometimes sensitive: they show actual places, on someone's land.

It makes sense to graduate access, per project.

Maps: the product

Made from the profiles in order to be used: planning, extension, research.

Published means visible and downloadable.

This is how OpenNSIS is designed: restrictions apply to profiles; a published map can always be downloaded.

FAIR: four questions to ask of the data

PrincipleThe questionWhat answers it
FindableCan someone from outside discover that these data exist?metadata + catalogue
AccessibleIs it known how to obtain them, even if on conditions?web services, a stable address
InteroperableDo they open in other software, with the same codes?OGC standards, GloSIS codes
ReusableIs it known what one is allowed to do with them?licence + origin of the data
FAIR is not the same as open: restricted data can, and should, be FAIR too.

A · Principles

As open as possible, as closed as necessary

Closed

Inside the institution only. Nobody outside knows it exists.

Public metadata

It is known that it exists, when it dates from and whom to ask. The data, on request.

Graduated sharing

Locations without attributes, blurred coordinates, a limited number.

Open

View and download, with a licence that says what is allowed.

The GSP data policy asks for the second step as a minimum: metadata open by default.

A · Principles

Whose data are they?

  • Sharing is not giving up ownership. Whoever licenses remains the holder.
  • Only whoever holds that right can license: check the agreements with projects, partners and funders.
  • The licence belongs to the institution, not to the technician who uploaded the file.
  • Personal data, such as farmers' names and contacts, are not published.

A licence takes nothing
from whoever grants it.
It gives certainty to whoever uses.

B · CC licences

Creative Commons in four elements

BY

Attribution

Credit must be given to the author.

SA

ShareAlike

Adaptations must be shared under the same terms.

NC

NonCommercial

Only non-commercial use is allowed.

ND

NoDerivatives

Adaptations and derived works are not allowed.

Free, standard licences, with three readings: for people, for lawyers and for machines · creativecommons.org

B · CC licences

Translated into soil data

BY: "cite us"

The map can be used, clipped and combined by anyone, provided they cite the institution.

SA: "what you make stays equally open"

A map derived from ours has to be published under the same licence.

NC: "not for business"

Excludes commercial uses, including input suppliers and agricultural advisory companies.

ND: "only as it is"

It can be viewed and redistributed; clips, reclassifications and combinations cannot be published.

"Open", in the sense of the Open Definition, means only CC0, CC BY and CC BY-SA.

B · CC licences

Four things to know before licensing

  • The licence is irrevocable: you can stop distributing, but you cannot withdraw what has already been given.
  • Only the rights holder can license.
  • The licence is not exclusive: the holder can grant other permissions, for example commercial use to a partner.
  • Version 4.0 also covers database rights.

QuestionIf "no"If "yes"

3Do we accept commercial use?add NCcontinue

Each "no" to questions 2 and 3 reduces who can use the data. It is worth asking: whom do we want to serve?

C · Choosing

An institutional policy on one page

  • The default licence for maps.
  • The rule for profiles: publish? with attributes? with blurring?
  • Who approves the publication of a dataset.
  • The institution's citation text.
  • The embargo period for data from ongoing projects and theses.
  • When what is published is reviewed.

A written rule
saves one discussion
for every map.

A topic to take to the governance sessions.

R · OpenNSIS

In OpenNSIS, the licence is part of the record

  • When uploading a raster or ingesting profiles, the licence is chosen from a list: the six CC licences, CC0 or public domain.
  • It is stored in the dataset's ISO 19115 metadata.
  • It appears in the map's metadata window, in the catalogue and in the XML, and travels on to the federation.

Published

R · OpenNSIS

Maps: what "publish" means

  • Publishing a raster: it becomes visible on the map, with a WMS service, a catalogue record and a GeoTIFF to download.
  • Unpublishing only hides it from the viewer: the WMS and the metadata record stay active.
  • To withdraw a map: delete it.
  • To not share a map: do not upload it to the SIS.

The SIS exists to
distribute the maps.
The licence says on what terms.

R · OpenNSIS

Good practice day to day

Before uploading

Confirm who holds the data and which licence was agreed with the project or the funder.

When uploading

Title, abstract, period, keywords, an institutional contact, and the licence. Two minutes.

Afterwards

At the end of each project, review what was published: licences, visible profiles, blurring.

The GloSIS federation receives exactly what the visitor sees; the same rules serve both.
