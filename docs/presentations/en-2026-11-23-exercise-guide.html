A · Profiles

The rules the validator applies

WhatRuleExample that fails
Datestrict format YYYY-MM-DD; always compulsory12/05/2024
Coordinatesdecimal degrees (or a national EPSG, stated at upload); ≥ 95% of the points inside the country-13.98, 33.60 (swapped)
Same profilerows with the same code have the same coordinatesone layer with another longitude
Depthscentimetres, whole numbers; upper < lower20 → 20
Continuityeach layer starts where the previous one ends0–5, 10–30 (gap)
Closed listsTrialPit | Borehole · Horizon | Layer, exactly as writtentrialpit
Valueswithin the limits of the property, after the unit is convertedpH 61
Only what passes every check gets in; there are no exceptions. Data are curated before the upload; the validator catches the last slips.

A · Profiles · Exercise 1 10 min

Create the group's project

  1. Go into the Admin Panel with the group's account.
  2. Open the Projects tab and click + New project.
  3. Project ID: capitals and digits only, for example GROUP1.
  4. Give it a name and a description: the description is the abstract the public will read.
  5. Under Authors, state the organisation and a person, with + Add author.
  6. Click Create project.

Done when

The project appears in the list, with 0 profiles and 0 rasters.

To think about

The ID cannot be changed later and goes into the name of every map. Which naming rule does the institution want?

A · Profiles · Exercise 2 20 min

Fill in the template

  1. Open the template and read the legend and the Validation rules sheet.
  2. Replace the examples with two profiles: one with three layers, another with two, at a place in Tunisia that you know.
  3. Add one column with another property, for example sand_pct.
  4. Delete the legend rows under the data: otherwise they go into the CSV.
  5. Save as CSV (UTF-8), separated by commas and with a decimal point.
  6. Open the CSV in a text editor and confirm what was written.

Done when

The first line of the CSV has the column names separated by commas and the date reads 2024-05-12.

Careful

Excel in French writes ; and a decimal comma. In LibreOffice, choose "," as the field separator when saving.

A · Profiles

Where it all happens: the "Soil profiles" tab

At the top, the CSV upload. Below, each uploaded file: Open to describe, map and validate it; Ingest once the validation is clean. The "Result" column counts what went in.

A · Profiles · Exercise 3 25 min

Upload the CSV and map the columns

  1. In Soil profiles: Choose file… and then Upload CSV.
  2. In the list, click Open and check the Preview: the right columns and rows?
  3. Under Metadata: the group's project, an abstract, the licence and the EPSG code (4326 for decimal degrees).
  4. Under Standardisation: choose the Destination of each CSV column.
  5. For the property columns: Property, Procedure and Unit.
  6. Click Save.

Done when

Every column has a destination, or (skip) if it is of no interest, and the mapping is saved.

To think about

What exactly is the laboratory's method? The procedure is part of the datum: without it, two pH values cannot be compared.

A · Profiles · Exercise 4 25 min

Validate, make a mistake on purpose, correct, ingest

  1. Click Validate and read the results: required destinations, country bounds, checks per column.
  2. Make a mistake on purpose: in the preview, click a cell and change a lower depth from 20 to 25. Validate again.
  3. Tick Only profiles with errors: which layers were flagged, and why both?
  4. Try another error: a pH of 61 or a date 12/05/2024.
  5. Correct everything, validate and, with a clean validation, ← Back to list and Ingest.
  6. Open the Map and click one of your profiles.

Done when

The "Result" column says how many rows, profiles, properties and measurements went in, and the profile opens on the map.

Challenge

Download the profile as CSV from the map and compare it with the file you uploaded: what changed in the carbon?

A · Profiles · Exercise 5 10 min

Decide what the public sees

  1. In Soil profiles, find the row of the group's project.
  2. Under Symbology, choose a shape and a colour of your own.
  3. Set Spatial blur to 1000 metres and see, on the map, where the points went.
  4. Change Share attributes to "No" and click a profile on the map: what is left?
  5. Set the Public limit to 1: how many profiles are visible now?
  6. Leave the combination the group would defend for real data.

Done when

The group can explain, in two sentences, the sharing rule it chose.

To think about

Data policy was discussed earlier. Which of these four levers matches each decision taken?

B · Maps · Exercise 6 20 min

Publish a map from a GeoTIFF

  1. In Rasters, open Upload GeoTIFF and choose the example file, or a small map of your own.
  2. State the group's project, the mapped soil property and the unit.
  3. Fill in the dates (created on; start and end of the period), the depth and the statistic (MEAN, SDEV or UNCT).
  4. Choose the licence and tick Publish to catalogue.
  5. Read the generated filename before clicking Upload.
  6. In the GeoTIFF's list: give it a display name and click Check WMS.

Done when

The map appears in the layer list of the Map, with a legend, and the "WMS" column is in order.

Challenge

Make the map the "Default" and change its opacity: what does someone opening the Map for the first time see now?

B · Maps · Exercise 7 15 min

From the metadata record to QGIS

  1. On the Map, open the metadata of the map you published: are title, abstract, licence and contact right?
  2. Download the XML and look for the licence inside it.
  3. In QGIS: Layer → Add Layer → Add WMS/WMTS Layer → New.
  4. Paste the map's WMS address, with the port of your instance and the identifier of your map (the "Raster ID" column of the GeoTIFF's list).
    Example: group 01, map TN-GROUP1-TEXTCLAY-2026-0-30-MEAN:
    http://5.223.62.255:8011/mapserver?map=/etc/mapserver/TN-GROUP1-TEXTCLAY-2026-0-30-MEAN.map
  5. Connect, choose the layer and Add.

Done when

The group's map is open in QGIS, over a base map, without having downloaded any file.

Challenge

Open the map of another group in QGIS in the same way. What did you need them to give you?

B · Maps · Exercise 8 15 min

Announce the data: a news post on the website

  1. In Website, in the news section, click New post.
  2. Write the title, the author and the date, and one or two tags.
  3. In the body, in Markdown: what was published, whose data they are, under which licence, how to cite.
  4. Save first as a Draft and read it with a visitor's eyes.
  5. Change it to Published and open the News page.

Done when

The group's post is on the public page and another person, with no context, understands what they can do with the data.

Challenge

In the same tab, change the title, the image and the colours of the site: the instance belongs to the group.

C · Server · Exercise 9 25 min

Make a backup and test it

# 1 · log in to the server
ssh root@5.223.62.255
# 2 · the database
cd /opt/sis-tn01
docker exec sis-tn01-database pg_dump -U sis -d sis \
  | gzip > sis-db-$(date +%F).sql.gz
# 3 · the files: maps, catalogue, configuration
tar czf sis-volumes-$(date +%F).tgz \
  sis-web-services/volume sis-metadata/volume .env
# 4 · check
ls -lh sis-db-* sis-volumes-*
# 5 · leave the server; on the laptop, fetch the copies
exit
scp 'root@5.223.62.255:/opt/sis-tn01/sis-*-20*' \
  ~/Downloads/
ls -lh ~/Downloads/sis-*-20*

One volunteer at the keyboard, the screen projected. The example is group 01's; on a national installation the names are /opt/sis and sis-database.

Done when

There are two files with today's date, of plausible size, and a copy of both off the server, in the laptop's Downloads folder.

Challenge

Prove that the group's data are in there:
zcat sis-db-*.sql.gz | grep -c GROUP1

C · Server · Exercise 10 20 min

Update the system and check that all is well

  1. In Administration, section Software update: note the installed version and click Check for updates.
  2. Confirm that there is a backup from today (exercise 9).
  3. On the server, update:
    cd /opt/sis-tn01 && ./update.sh
  4. See the state of the services: are they all running?
    docker ps --filter name=sis-tn01
  5. In the browser: the map opens, a profile opens, a map is drawn, the version changed.
  6. Record in the system log book: date, version, who did it, what was checked.

Done when

The four checks pass and there is a new line in the system log book.

To think about

This, plus the backup, is the monthly routine: about four hours. Whose task is it, and who stands in?

R · National data · Exercise 11 60 min

Now with your own data

  1. Choose a real and small dataset: twenty profiles teach more than two thousand.
  2. Move it into the template: one row per layer, one code per profile.
  3. Check at the source: coordinates and their system, dates, depths in centimetres, units.
  4. Note, for each property, the laboratory method.
  5. Upload, map, validate and ingest into the group's project.
  6. Keep the list of what had to be corrected: it is the start of your curation procedure.

Done when

At least one national dataset passed validation, or you know exactly why it did not.

No data at hand?

Work with another group, or repeat exercise 2 with the data of a survey report on paper.

R · National data

Tidying up

Profiles

In Soil profiles, the Prune button removes the project's profiles from the database. The project and the uploaded CSV files stay.

Maps

In Rasters, Delete removes in one go the file, the map, the catalogue record and the database entries.

Project and files

Deleting a project or an uploaded CSV is an administrator task; on your instance, that is the group.

Unpublishing hides; deleting removes. The training instances are temporary: what you take home is your corrected CSV and the list of what had to be corrected.
