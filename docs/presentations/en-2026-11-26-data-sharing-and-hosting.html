A · Needs

What is needed to install

ResourceMinimumRecommended
Processor2 vCPU4 vCPU
Memory4 GB8 GB
Disk40 GB SSD100 GB SSD or more
Operating system64-bit Linux with long-term support
SoftwareDocker and Git; everything else comes in containers
  • A fixed public IP, with ports 80 and 443 open.
  • A domain name of the institution; HTTPS is then automatic.
  • No licences to pay: every piece is free software.
A national SIS is small. Measured on the Tunisia demonstration instance: about 0.4 GB of memory at rest, 47 MB of maps and 23 MB of database.

B · Install

Installing is one command

# on an empty Linux server
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/un-fao/
OpenNSIS/main/ops/single/host-install.sh \
  | sudo bash -s -- TN
# later, to update
cd /opt/sis && ./update.sh

The country code at the end sets the country; the installer does the rest in about ten minutes.

  • It installs Docker, sets the firewall, fetches the code, creates the database and starts the system.
  • It prints the administrator password once, at the end.
  • An update never touches the data.
  • Keeping it running is about 4 hours a month for one person: updates and backups.

C · Sharing

The fears are legitimate, and they have an answer

FearAnswer
"We lose control of our data."The data stay on your server; the licence says what others may do.
"They use the data and do not cite us."Attribution is compulsory in every Creative Commons licence.
"Someone makes money from our work."Commercial use can be excluded, or authorised case by case.
"The data have errors and we will be criticised."The metadata state the method, the date and the quality.
"The points show someone's farm."Share only the locations, blur the coordinates or limit the number of profiles.
A soil profile costs fieldwork, transport and laboratory, once. Its value lies in being used many times.

C · Sharing

The switches are in your hands

Soil profiles: four controls per project

  • Published: the profiles appear on the public map, or not.
  • Share attributes: with the analytical data, or only the points.
  • Spatial blur: a random shift of the coordinates, in metres.
  • Public limit: the maximum number of profiles shown.

Maps: published with their licence

  • The licence is chosen from a list at upload and travels with the map.
  • Unpublishing hides a map from the viewer.
  • Deleting withdraws it completely.
  • A map you do not want to share is simply not uploaded.
Admin panel → Soil profiles and Rasters. Each control takes one click and has immediate effect.

C · Sharing

A licence protects the one who shares

BY

"Cite us"

Credit to the institution is compulsory. This one is in every licence.

NC

"Not for business"

Add it to exclude commercial use.

ND

"Only as it is"

Add it to forbid clipped or reworked versions.

SA

"Keep it open"

Add it so that derived maps carry the same licence.

Creative Commons licences are free and standard. Sharing is not giving up ownership: whoever licenses remains the holder of the data.

R · Recap

Two messages to take away

Installing is within reach

  • Three possible homes; the system is the same in each.
  • A modest Linux server, a public address, a domain name.
  • One command to install, one to update.
  • About four hours a month to keep it running.

Sharing is under your control

  • The data stay on your server.
  • You choose how open, layer by layer.
  • The licence states what others may do, and that they cite you.
  • The controls can be changed at any time.
